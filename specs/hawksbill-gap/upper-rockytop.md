# Upper Rockytop bedding comparison figure

This specification supports [issue #27](https://github.com/Moriae1337/martian-map/issues/27) ([CH26-009]), [issue #22](https://github.com/Moriae1337/martian-map/issues/22) ([CH26-004]), [issue #24](https://github.com/Moriae1337/martian-map/issues/24) ([CH26-006]), and links to the [Skinner Ridge science stop](skinner-ridge.md), the [Skinner Ridge contact observation specification](skinner-ridge-observation.md), and the [Hawksbill Gap crew field objective](field-objective.md).

It documents Supplementary Figure S3 from Williams et al. (2026), which provides the primary comparative sedimentological evidence used to reclassify the Skinner Ridge rock block as a displaced float block derived from the Upper Rockytop member rather than in-place Lower Rockytop bedrock.

---

## Geological context and significance

During its investigation of the Jezero crater delta front at Hawksbill Gap, the Mars 2020 Perseverance rover examined an isolated ~1 m rock block designated **Skinner Ridge** at the base of the Rockytop scarp (sols 488–496). Two rock cores were drilled and sealed here: **Swift Run** (sample #10, sol 490) and **Skyland** (sample #11, sol 495), alongside the **Thornton Gap** abrasion patch (sol 482 / sol 493 SuperCam observation).

The stratigraphic affinity of Skinner Ridge is the subject of competing published interpretations:

1. **In-place Lower Rockytop member bedrock:** The initial mission science team report ([PDS Initial Reports Volume 2 Release 2, Section 11][pds]) classified the block as likely in-place bedrock based on bedding strike and dip paralleling the surrounding Rockytop scarp, and the mechanical stability of the block during heavy rotary-percussive coring and abrasion.
2. **Displaced Upper Rockytop float block:** Williams et al. (2026), in the section "Classification of Rockytop float rocks", reclassified Skinner Ridge as an allochthonous float block shed from the stratigraphically higher Upper Rockytop facies through mass wasting. Their argument relies on visual bedding architecture, weathering profile, and geochemical affinities (SuperCam LIBS and PIXL spectra matching nearby float blocks like Betty's Rock).

**Supplementary Figure S3** is the central visual document underpinning the displaced float hypothesis. It provides a direct, high-resolution visual comparison of the bedding architecture and weathering resistance profiles among three rock exposures.

### 1. The three photographic views

Supplementary Figure S3 organizes three paired, scale-calibrated photographic panels acquired by the rover's Mastcam-Z and Remote Micro-Imager (SuperCam RMI) instruments:

1. **View 1: Upper Rockytop reference facies (In-place bedrock)**
   - **Target / Location:** In-situ Upper Rockytop member exposed along the elevated cliff-forming scarp of the Shenandoah formation, stratigraphically above the Hawksbill Gap traverse floor.
   - **Bedding architecture:** Rhythmic, planar-to-wavy laminations exhibiting differential cementation and competence. Resistant sandy beds protrude as distinct horizontal ribs, while weaker silty/sandy intervals weather recessively.
   - **Morphological role:** Establishes the type bedding and weathering profile of intact Upper Rockytop strata.

2. **View 2: Betty's Rock float block (Recognized detached float)**
   - **Target / Location:** Betty's Rock, an unambiguous detached float boulder situated downslope along the delta front near Hawksbill Gap.
   - **Bedding architecture:** Exhibits prominent stacked, protruding resistant ribs separated by recessive erodible grooves, matching the Upper Rockytop scarp ledges.
   - **Compositional role:** Serves as the compositional and sedimentological bridge. The initial mission report and subsequent geochemical analyses confirm Betty's Rock as Upper Rockytop float; SuperCam and PIXL analyses at Thornton Gap (Skinner Ridge) closely match Betty's Rock elemental concentrations.

3. **View 3: Skinner Ridge block face (Disputed ~1 m block)**
   - **Target / Location:** The exposed southern face of the ~1 m Skinner Ridge block (`18.458931° N, 77.40617078° E`), adjacent to the Swift Run / Skyland drill holes and Thornton Gap abrasion patch.
   - **Bedding architecture:** Exposes fine-to-medium grained sandstone with planar laminations (~1 cm or thinner individual laminae). Crucially, the weathered surface demonstrates the same rhythmically corrugated, ribbed expression as Upper Rockytop bedrock and Betty's Rock.

### 2. Standardized ~2 cm scale bars

A key methodological strength of Supplementary Figure S3 is the inclusion of standardized **~2 cm scale bars** across all three views:

- **Metric normalization:** Because raw spacecraft images have differing standoffs and camera focal lengths, raw perspective can mislead geological interpretations. The ~2 cm scale bars normalize all three images to an identical spatial scale.
- **Lamina and rib quantification:** At the ~2 cm scale, individual protruding resistant beds have thicknesses between 0.5 cm and 2.0 cm, separated by similarly proportioned recessive interbeds.
- **Reference for crew field photography:** For human exploration (Hawksbill Gap crew field objective), Figure S3 defines the scale standard for surface photography. Astronauts utilize a 2 cm gnomon scale bar on the geological scale stick, positioned perpendicular to bedding strike, to collect directly comparable field photographs.

### 3. Alternating protruding and recessive layering

The diagnostic diagnostic feature documented in Figure S3 is **differential weathering relief**:

- **Protruding layers (Resistant ribs):** More indurated, competent sandstone layers that resist aeolian abrasion (sand-blasting by wind-driven grains) and thermal fatigue. These form protruding ledges, fins, or ribs extending several millimeters to centimeters outward from the rock surface.
- **Recessive layers (Undercut grooves):** Less cemented, finer-grained, or more friable laminae that erode preferentially under Martian wind and saltation, forming narrow, indented horizontal troughs between protruding ribs.
- **Rhythmic repetition:** The alternating protruding and recessive layers repeat rhythmically through the vertical thickness of the block, forming a characteristic corrugated profile.
- **Contrast with Lower Rockytop bedrock (Williams et al., Fig. 3C):**
  - In-place Lower Rockytop bedrock surrounding Skinner Ridge is light-toned, thin-bedded, and recessive/slope-forming.
  - Lower Rockytop exposures lack prominent resistant protruding ribs, weathering instead into subdued, crumbling, smooth slopes or platy pavements.
  - This stark sedimentological contrast led Williams et al. to conclude that Skinner Ridge could not be in-place Lower Rockytop.

## Critical geological distinction: Facies match vs. structural contact

While Supplementary Figure S3 establishes a compelling facies resemblance between Skinner Ridge and Upper Rockytop, mission geologists and field crews must maintain rigorous distinction between facies resemblance and physical contact continuity:

1. **Parallel bedding ambiguity:**
   - Skinner Ridge's bedding strikes ~082° and dips ~3.5° south, exactly paralleling the regional Rockytop scarp.
   - However, a coherent rock block that slumps, slides, or detaches along a low-angle failure plane or colluvial slope can easily translate downslope without rotating or tilting.
   - Therefore, parallel bedding fits **both** hypotheses: intact bedrock, and a translationally slumped float block. Parallel attitude cannot settle the question.

2. **Facies resemblance is supporting, not decisive:**
   - Figure S3 proves that Skinner Ridge shares weathering style, bed spacing, and competence with Upper Rockytop.
   - However, sedimentary facies can recur within a basin due to cyclical depositional processes. Resemblance alone cannot demonstrate that gravity transport occurred.

3. **Contact observation decides:**
   - As specified in [skinner-ridge-observation.md](skinner-ridge-observation.md) ([CH26-006]), the deciding observation is the physical contact at the block margins.
   - If individual beds trace continuously across the margin into adjacent Lower Rockytop bedrock, the block is **in-place bedrock** (`continuous`), overriding the facies resemblance.
   - If the basal underside rests on unconsolidated regolith/colluvium or beds truncate discordantly, the block is **float** (`separated`), confirming the Figure S3 hypothesis.
   - If the contact is banked with loose regolith or shadowed, the outcome remains strictly **`unresolved`** (defer rather than guess).
